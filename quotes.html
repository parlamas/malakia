Herodotus, Histories, Book IX, 122.2–3 (the closing anecdote of the entire work) The actual Greek, confirmed: φιλέειν γὰρ ἐκ τῶν μαλακῶν χώρων μαλακοὺς ἄνδρας γίνεσθαι — "for soft men tend to come from soft lands" (literally: "it is customary that from soft regions soft men come to be"). This is Cyrus's reply when the Persians propose leaving their small, harsh homeland (γῆν τρηχέαν — "rugged land," 9.122.2) for richer territory. Cyrus rejects the idea on the grounds that soft land breeds soft men, and — per the sources I found — it is literally the very last sentence of Herodotus's entire Histories: the Persians heed Cyrus and choose to stay rulers on rugged land rather than become subjects in a fertile one.
